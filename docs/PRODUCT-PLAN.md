# Product plan: what would make RevBot sell

From the research run of 2026-10-09 (details and sources in [MARKET-RESEARCH.md](MARKET-RESEARCH.md)). This is the judge's merged plan. It disagrees with parts of Dave's own direction in places, and says so; the call stays Dave's.

## The thesis

Sell RevBot as a cute physical desk pet that stays quiet until it has a reason to speak, paid once, with chat as a bundled feature and the Claude Code terminal mod and PowerShell module as the free funnel. The evidence does not show anyone paying for chat personality: Microsoft removed Mico from Copilot voice in August 2026, Grok's companions lifted iOS revenue 9 percent, and AI chat pets on Steam rate 52 to 75 percent positive, mostly on hardware complaints. What the evidence shows people pay for is a cheap, passive, charming thing beside their work: Rusty's Retirement ($6.99, 97 percent), Chillquarium ($5.99, 93), MateEngine ($5.49, 97) and Wallpaper Engine ($4.99, 97). The Claude Code pet slot is crowded and mostly free (clawd-on-desk 6,402 stars and 27 agents, first-party Codex pets, Petdex 4,876 pets, about two dozen new Clawd band mods), so hooks are table stakes, not a moat. What RevBot has that none of those is shown to have is the hand-built physical toy (throw, squash, dizzy, climb windows) plus one identity across desktop, terminal and shell. The anti-Clippy pitch (it shuts up when told) is cheap to build and matches the documented Clippy failures. The biggest gap is not a feature: there is no installer, no signing, no one-click setup (the README has users hand-edit hook entries into ~/.claude/settings.json, and settings.json and state.json sit beside the exe). Honest outlook: Dave has no audience and all Revenant products have zero sales; Gamalytic estimates 65.9 percent of 2025 Steam releases grossed under $1,000, no source gives a conversion rate for any desktop pet, and Bongo Cat's 194,000 concurrent peak paid its developer a self-reported $2,000 to $4,050 a month. Plan for hundreds of dollars to low thousands in year one; 1,000 buyers is the upside case, and a clip that spreads the way Desktop Goose's did is luck the plan only gives more chances. Dave's goal of an easy chat companion stays in the product as a one-keystroke chat box, but it keeps people, it does not make them buy.

## Do not build (yet)

- Telegram bot and remote approve or deny from a phone. No sales evidence for phone companions, Dave's stated goal is a companion on the computer, clawd-on-desk already offers it free, and remote approval makes a leaked bot token a path to approving tool calls on the user's PC. If buyers ask later: owner-only chat-id allowlist, notify-only first, approval opt-in per session with a timeout (https://github.com/rullerzhou-afk/clawd-on-desk).
- A bundled or fine-tuned 1 to 3B local LLM persona as a headline feature now. Published CPU speeds for Llama 3.2 3B span 0.24 to 23.81 tok/s across 13 systems against about 5 tok/s needed to read along, Google says Gemma 3 270M is not designed for complex conversation, and the Steam AI pets rate 52 to 75 percent with VRAM complaints. Keep Ollama and endpoints bring-your-own, ship hand-written quips, and revisit only with real first-run speed-test data (https://www.promptquorum.com/local-llms/fastest-local-llms-low-end-pcs and https://developers.googleblog.com/en/introducing-gemma-3-270m/ and https://store.steampowered.com/app/4227700/AI_Desktop_Pet/).
- Gacha, random drops, tradeable items, season or Paw-style passes, paid timers. Bongo Cat's pass and drop rates are its main negative theme, the drop economy drew bots by the developer's account, and a 194,000 concurrent peak earned a self-reported $2,000 to $4,050 a month (https://store.steampowered.com/appreviews/3419430?json=1&filter=all&review_type=negative&language=english&num_per_page=20&day_range=90 and https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/one-of-steams-most-popular-games-is-actually-losing-the-devs-money-3220149/).
- Free-to-play for reach. Reach is not revenue for a solo developer without licensed IP (same Bongo Cat sources above).
- Per-character DLC at about $15, or removing user content after shipping it. Desktop Mate has 42 DLCs, is Mixed at 61 percent, 18 of 20 top negatives cite price and 16 cite removed modding (https://store.steampowered.com/app/3301060/Desktop_Mate/).
- A subscription, credits, or a hosted backend that routes users through anyone's Claude Pro or Max login. No server cost justifies a subscription when chat runs on the user's own login, key or Ollama; Grok's companions behind a $30 tier moved revenue 9 percent; Anthropic's legal page bars routing through Free, Pro or Max credentials (https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/21/groks-ai-companions-drove-downloads-but-its-latest-model-is-the-one-making-money and https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance).
- Screen vision, always-listening wake words, continuous screen commentary, and any keystroke hook. Opinion is split even among paid-AI-pet reviewers (one wasted money on a misread Google Doc), 8 GB VRAM comes with it, and Recall needed an opt-in redesign. If ever added: one user-triggered, local, single-use look-at-this-window hotkey, not in the next six months (https://steamcommunity.com/app/4227700/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated and https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/privacy-and-control-over-your-recall-experience-d404f672-7647-41e5-886c-a3c59680af15).
- Timer-driven chatter, 'it looks like you are doing X' tips, or any prompt that returns after dismissal. That is the documented Clippy failure (https://xenon.stanford.edu/~lswartz/paperclip/paperclip.pdf).
- Romance or companion roleplay, accounts, age fields, or any server holding user chats; and do not bundle the planned kid's edition (Desk-Pet-Maker) or add chat or data collection to it. Bonzi paid $75,000 over children's data, Replika was fined EUR 5M (under appeal), Character.AI ended open-ended chat for minors (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2004/02/umg-recordings-inc-pay-400000-bonzi-software-inc-pay-75000-settle-coppa-civil-penalty-charges and https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/29/character-ai-is-killing-the-chatbot-experience-for-minors/).
- Detachable arm, mech mode as a game mechanic, the walk-into-the-terminal hand-off, and the web or LAN phone body, before launch. No source shows demand for any of them, they are art-bound or are Dave's thesis rather than a demand signal, and the terminal mod and PowerShell module already cover the multi-body story. Ship mech only as a later cosmetic skin (https://store.steampowered.com/app/3419430/Bongo_Cat/).
- Hardware (a pendant or ESP32 desk buddy) and a mobile app. Humane sold to HP for $116M after shutdown, Friend was defaced across a $1M campaign, Rabbit's founder cited about 5 percent daily use, and Anthropic already published an ESP32 reference (https://the-decoder.com/humane-ends-its-ai-pin-in-ten-days-sells-technology-to-hp-for-116-million/ and https://github.com/anthropics/claude-desktop-buddy).
- A Mac or Linux port before there are sales. Months of work on a layered-window WinForms app, nothing in the evidence shows demand for this product there, and clawd-on-desk already covers those platforms free (https://github.com/rullerzhou-afk/clawd-on-desk).
- Anthropic's Clawd art, 'Claude Code' or Anthropic in a product name or logo, and Microsoft's Clippy art or name in marketing. Anthropic's legal page restricts names and logos, and the Clippy character is Microsoft's, used by the free Rieseberg app only under a fair-use claim. Say 'works with Claude Code' in plain text and keep RevBot's own art (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance and https://github.com/felixrieseberg/clippy).
- Your own pet marketplace with payments. Petdex and codex-pet.org already host free galleries, and taking money adds merchant-of-record and tax work (https://github.com/crafter-station/petdex and https://stripe.com/pricing).
- Adapters for many agents to match a '27 agents' claim, a work-along focus timer, and a pet gallery or catalog. Breadth races are lost by a solo developer, and the timer's evidence is only a store-page listing plus praise for company while working (https://github.com/rullerzhou-afk/clawd-on-desk and https://store.steampowered.com/app/2666510/Rustys_Retirement/).
- A Steam launch with no wishlist runway. At the surveyed 0.1 to 0.2 first-week copies per launch wishlist, a page with no wishlists sells almost nothing (https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/steam-the-new-wishlists-to-first).

## Pricing

$6.99 one-time on Steam, no subscription, no pass, no per-character DLC, with a 20 percent launch discount ($5.59). The terminal mod and PowerShell module stay free, and a RevBot-only demo goes on itch.io as the wishlist feeder. Reasoning: the verified paid, well-reviewed comparators cluster at $4.99 to $6.99 (Wallpaper Engine $4.99, MateEngine $5.49, Chillquarium $5.99, Rusty's Retirement $6.99) and CodeMon is $5 or $8; review risk rises above that (AI Desktop Pet at $12.99 is 75 percent positive on hardware complaints; Spirit City at $11.99 is 97 percent but has far more content). Rusty's $6.99 is the closest template for a paid-once passive companion, and RevBot's chat, Claude Code integration and terminal body justify the top of the cluster, not above it. The evidence does not separate $5.99 from $7.99, so treat $6.99 as a judgment call and test the discount. I rejected a full pay-what-you-want core build: itch.io's own 2015 average donation was $3.68, and whether MateEngine's free build helps or hurts its Steam sales is unknown. Cosmetic packs later at $2.99 to $4.99, also earnable. Arithmetic, not a forecast: at 30 percent Steam cut a $6.99 sale nets about $4.89 before tax and refunds, 1,000 buyers is about $6,990 gross and about $4,890 net, and netting $10,000 needs roughly 2,050 sales. At the surveyed 0.1 to 0.2 first-week copies per launch wishlist, 1,000 wishlists is roughly 100 to 200 first-week copies. Rusty's 550,000 copies is a self-reported outlier, not a plan. Expect a first-year side income, not a salary.

## Distribution

Steam is the paid channel, free surfaces feed it, and a clean direct install sits behind it. Every verified paid desk-companion sale in the evidence happened on Steam, and the Desktop Companion tag holds only 273 apps (top-heavy: Bongo Cat about 30,200 reviews, the next about 9,100). Sequence: (1) Week 1, Dave's accounts and money, not the agent's: pay the $100 Steam app fee (recoupable after $1,000 of revenue; first-release wait is 21 to 30 days depending on the Valve page, unresolved until checked in Steamworks), put up a Coming Soon page with a 20 to 30 second throw, climb and dizzy clip, and start signing identity validation. (2) Weeks 1 to 4: release the free terminal mod and PowerShell module on GitHub with a GIF and a link to the desktop pet, using the original character; apply to Petdex's built-with list; post one honest Show HN and treat HN and Product Hunt as lotteries (the Clippy Show HN reached 1,122 points but its repo has stalled at 1,378 stars with a last push in November 2025; PeonPing got 117 Product Hunt points but only 1 to 4 on each HN submission); cut five or six 10-second clips for X, TikTok and Reddit, since Desktop Goose's spread came through other people's posts; put the RevBot-only demo on itch.io. Reddit was unreachable in the research, so r/ClaudeAI and other subreddits are unassessed. (3) Launch on Steam when wishlists and the bug backlog justify it, not on a calendar date; budget a bug-fix month afterward (multi-monitor and DPI support load) before adding features, and keep a visible monthly changelog because reviewers in this category fear abandoned solo-dev apps. (4) Fallbacks: an itch.io paid listing set to 'collected by itch.io' so itch.io is merchant of record and remits VAT, for buyers outside Steam; a Microsoft Store MSIX build later as a trust channel once Company account verification clears (free re-signing, no SmartScreen warning; whether the 100 percent commerce rule covers a Store-priced download is unverified). Whether Steam-delivered executables avoid SmartScreen warnings is unverified, so sign the binary anyway (Azure Artifact Signing about $9.99 a month; individuals are limited to the USA and Canada and LLC eligibility is unverified; reputation still takes weeks). Skip Gumroad, Patreon and self-run Stripe: more tax work for no extra reach. Before charging anyone, Dave should get written clarification from Anthropic on a paid product that drives a user's own claude binary.

## Where the strategists disagreed, and the ruling

- Headline pitch. The strategists agree chat is not what sells, but Dave's stated goal is a chat companion. Ruled: sell the calm physical desk pet that shuts up when told, keep chat as a bundled, one-keystroke feature. Evidence: AI chat pets on Steam rate 52 to 75 percent, Mico was removed from Copilot voice, and the paid hits are passive and cheap.
- What ranks first. One plan ranked the installer first, one the quiet contract, one the physical body. Ruled: the installer and Connect wizard rank first because nothing can be sold or even tried without them, the built physical toy is second as the asset, and the quiet contract is third but is the first thing built tonight because it costs days and needs no outside party. Signing and Steam paperwork have calendar lead times and start in week one.
- Is answering permissions from the pet built. One plan called it built, one called it unbuilt. Ruled by reading the repo: the Yes/No PermissionPrompt serves only the pet's own chat Claude; external sessions only raise the hand. The hand-up alert is ranked as built (rank 4), and answering external sessions is a separate unbuilt, security-sensitive item (rank 10), parity with free clawd-on-desk.
- Telegram bot. Excluded by one plan, ranked 14th and 9th by the others. Ruled: do not build now; no sales evidence, not the stated goal, and remote approval is a security surface. Notify-only and owner-only if buyers ask.
- Bundled local LLM persona. One plan ranked it 8th as a CPU hedge. Ruled: not now. CPU speeds vary 100-fold, Google says tiny models are not for conversation, and Ollama plus any OpenAI-compatible endpoint plus a no-AI mode already cover the hedge. Revisit with speed-test data from real users.
- Price. $7.99 launching at $5.99, $5.99 launching at $4.79, and $6.99 with a $9.99 ceiling. Ruled: $6.99 with a 20 percent launch discount. The evidence cannot separate $5.99 from $7.99, so this is a judgment call anchored on Rusty's Retirement.
- Free build. One plan proposed a pay-what-you-want core build on itch.io, another a free demo. Ruled: a RevBot-only demo, not a full pay-what-you-want build, because pay-what-you-want averaged $3.68 in itch.io's own data and the effect of MateEngine's free build on its sales is unknown.
- Claude Code dependence and Anthropic terms. One plan wanted API-key mode as the default, another treated Ollama as the hedge. Ruled: all brains are equal options in a first-run chooser, the product must not depend on any one, and Dave gets written clarification from Anthropic before charging. The mod's plugin.json name is 'revbot', which fits the naming rule; any store listing must keep Anthropic and Claude Code out of the product name and logo.
- Memory versus progression order. Two plans put memory first, one put progression first. Ruled: variety and memory first (cheaper, feeds chat and the privacy story), earned offline progression second, because demand for progression is large in reactions but unproven in purchases and both address a real novelty-decay problem.
- Pet import timing. Ranked 6th by one plan, 10th to 11th by the others. Ruled: 11th. It is weeks of C# work plus IP moderation and adds little before there are buyers; Workshop only after sales prove an audience.
- Other agents. Adapters for Codex CLI and others were ranked 8th by one plan and excluded by another. Ruled: document the generic pipe verbs now, build no adapters until users ask.
- Steam timing. All three agree Steam is the paid channel; they differ on how soon. Ruled: start the Steam page and signing clock now, launch only when wishlists and the bug backlog justify it, because at 0.1 to 0.2 first-week copies per wishlist a page with no audience sells almost nothing.
